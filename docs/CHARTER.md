# HAUNTED BLENDER — AAA MOONSHOT CHARTER

Status: ambitious destination; implementation scope is separately evidenced. AAA describes depth of creative control, durable projects and a complete filmmaker experience, not a guaranteed output quality or delivery date.

## North star
A filmmaker with a modest computer, an idea and 10,000 already-edited photographs can create, revise and distribute a coherent cinematic story. The same project may mature from a photo storyboard to filmed drama, episodic production and 3D virtual filmmaking without abandoning scene history.

## Non-negotiable laws
1. Story identity survives realization. A scene is a durable creative object; a render is one version of it, not the source of truth.
2. Originals are preserved. A Canon CR2 with a Lightroom XMP sidecar is **not** the edited/exported image; source, sidecar, derivative, crop and output remain individually traceable.
3. Local-first and file-owner-controlled. Import is not permission to upload, train, publish or perform biometric identity detection. Remote or paid generation needs an informed, per-operation authorization.
4. Human filmmaker controls character, drama, source use and export. AI suggestions are proposals, not observations or facts about people.
5. Versioned snapshots and adapters; preview, render and receipt must refer to the same accepted scene and shot semantics.
6. Distinguish observed provenance, user assertion, derived claim, fiction, reconstruction and unresolved uncertainty. A photograph does not prove a depicted person's identity, intentions, consent or life history.
7. A failure or partial job cannot claim success. Frozen work may be recovered and replayed, and editing one scene must not silently replace approved unrelated scenes.
8. Readable, inspectable project state, bounded CPU/RAM/disk use and repairable local data precede visual sophistication. Never sacrifice archives for a demo.

## Progressive workspaces — one project model
- **Spark:** idea + media → directed narrated photographic story.
- **Scene:** screenplay, beats, characters, location and shot direction; edit a scene independently.
- **Studio:** reusable cast/locations, takes, continuity, multi-track picture/audio, episodes.
- **Stage:** authored environments, depth/parallax, 3D, lighting, camera and motion rigs.
- **Production:** teamwork, licensing, approvals, episodic assets, reliable exports and delivery.

The interface may expose progressively greater complexity; underlying data must not fork into five unrelated ontologies.

## Core use cases
Independent drama, including Paula-style dialogue-driven episodic projects; personal photographic filmmaking from a large Lightroom/C​​anon archive; sourced documentary with explicit reconstruction labels; cross-application music-film collaborations with Haunted Toaster.

## Moonshot success
An independent filmmaker goes from idea and personal source archive to a watchable, editable short drama; revises scene two without rerendering scenes one and three; reopens the project; preserves source, rights, cost and execution provenance; can later graduate the same story to richer visual technologies.
